## 1. PREAMBLE

The `Xnegligence` repository was created to document the gap between what platforms promise and what they enforce, with a specific focus on ban evasion, broken enforcement, and absent support. The repository established a public clock: **January 1, 2027**, as the deadline for X to demonstrate a material change in its security posture.

This document does not claim credit for the actions of governments, regulators, or X itself. Instead, it serves as a factual record of the **measurable acceleration of enforcement** that has occurred since the repository was published, and it acknowledges the **observable steps X has taken** that align—directly or indirectly—with the core demands of this archive.

The goal of this update is to document the emerging **systemic preemption**: the world is moving to close the gaps that allowed hostile ecosystems to operate with impunity, and X is being compelled to participate in that closure.

## 2. THE EXTERNAL PRESSURE: GOVERNMENT AND REGULATORY ACTIONS AGAINST X

Since the repository's publication, X has faced a sustained and escalating wave of government and regulatory pressure. This pressure is not incidental; it is a direct response to the platform's failure to enforce its own rules at scale.

### 2.1 European Union – Digital Services Act (DSA) Enforcement

The European Commission has moved from investigation to financial consequences.

*   **Landmark Fine:** X was hit with a **€120 million fine** under the DSA, the first-ever such penalty under the bloc's landmark regulation[reference:0]. X has appealed the fine, but the financial and reputational pressure remains[reference:1].
*   **Accepted Remediation Plan:** On **July 15, 2026**, the European Commission **accepted X's action plan** to comply with transparency and researcher data access obligations under the DSA[reference:2]. This acceptance is a formal acknowledgment that X is now on a regulated path to compliance.
*   **Financial Guarantees:** The Commission has secured **bulletproof financial guarantees** from X to pay future DSA fines, ensuring that financial penalties are not an empty threat[reference:3].

### 2.2 United Kingdom – Ofcom and DSIT Pressure

The UK has applied its own regulatory muscle, focusing on illegal and harmful content.

*   **Ofcom Commitments:** On **May 15, 2026**, the UK's Office of Communications (Ofcom) **accepted a set of commitments from X** relating to the handling of illegal hate and terrorist content affecting UK users[reference:4].
*   **Parliamentary Pressure:** In the UK Parliament, MPs have explicitly stated that X is "afraid of the sanctions that will come next," and that X "has blinked" in response to regulatory pressure[reference:5]. The Secretary of State noted that fines can reach **up to 10% of worldwide qualifying revenue**[reference:6].
*   **Formal Investigation:** Ofcom has an active investigation into X over its alleged failure to protect users and children from illegal and harmful content[reference:7].

### 2.3 United States – Congressional and FTC Scrutiny

While the US pressure is more fragmented, it is present and increasing.

*   **Congressional Oversight:** The US House Judiciary Committee has demanded that X (along with Meta, Microsoft, and TikTok) **preserve and produce communications** with EU officials regarding DSA enforcement, indicating that US lawmakers are actively scrutinizing the platform's compliance behavior[reference:8].
*   **FTC Oversight:** Advocacy groups have urged the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) to **maintain and strengthen oversight** of X, opposing the platform's request to terminate FTC data processing audits[reference:9].

## 3. X'S OBSERVABLE COMPLIANCE SIGNALS: WHAT HAS CHANGED

In parallel with the external pressure, X has taken a series of public, observable steps that align with the core demands of the `Xnegligence` archive. These steps do not represent a full resolution of the documentation gap, but they are **material changes** that must be recorded.

### 3.1 Ban Evasion Enforcement: Policy and Practice

The repository's primary demand has been the enforcement of X's own ban evasion policy. X has now demonstrated both a policy commitment and operational action.

*   **Explicit Policy:** X's Authenticity policy explicitly states: **"Ban Evasion: You may not circumvent X enforcement actions."** It further states that X may suspend **"any other account it believes the same holder operates, regardless of when the other account was created"**[reference:10][reference:11].
*   **Operational Enforcement:** X has conducted **mass suspensions** for "inauthentic behaviors," with product lead Nikita Bier confirming a new spam filter was deployed and that accounts not operated by a human tapping the screen—including **associated accounts**—would be suspended[reference:12][reference:13]. This represents a direct operationalization of ban evasion and coordinated inauthentic behavior enforcement.

### 3.2 Transparency: "Under the Hood" and Open-Source Moderation

The repository demanded transparency. X has responded with an unprecedented level of public disclosure.

*   **Under the Hood:** X launched and expanded the **"Under the Hood"** transparency tool. Users can now see when a **government authority** seeks the removal of a post or the restriction of an account, including the government agency involved and the legal grounds provided[reference:14]. Accounts meeting certain criteria can download their own moderation data[reference:15].
*   **Open-Source Moderation Stack:** On **August 13, 2026**, X open-sourced its **entire moderation and visibility stack**, growing its public repository from approximately 216 files to **2,015 files across 25 services**[reference:16]. This includes the systems behind visibility filtering, abuse enforcement, bot detection (`botmaker`), and the "Under the Hood" report itself[reference:17]. X has explicitly stated that the code "can be audited; it can be critiqued"[reference:18].

### 3.3 Safety and Enforcement: Acknowledging the Gaps

X has not claimed perfection. The platform has acknowledged that its automated enforcement has, at times, been overzealous, and it has taken corrective action.

*   **Bug Fixes:** When a new spam filter falsely flagged a subset of accounts, X's product lead confirmed the bug was corrected and **99% of affected accounts were reinstated**[reference:19].
*   **Withholding Systems to Prevent Evasion:** X has stated that it **withheld certain systems** (e.g., Grok-based predictive systems) to **stop bad actors from evading enforcement**[reference:20]. This is a direct acknowledgment that evasion is a threat and that X is actively managing it.
